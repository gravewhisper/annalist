The Reed Pond

Tamsin Vey was fifteen the spring she knelt at the edge of the reed pond of the Silver Reed Sect, with mud to her elbows and a bucket of frogs she had been told to count. The sect sat in a valley of tall grey reeds that whispered all night, and every disciple began, as she had, by wading.

Elder Kovic Brannoch found her there at noon. He was a short, broad man with a beard like a hedge, and he carried a reed flute he never played.

"Sit," he said, and she sat in the mud. "Breathe the way I showed you. Draw the mist off the water and hold it below your navel."

She breathed. The mist rose from the pond in slow threads and curled into her, cool and heavy, and settled where he had said.

"Good. You know what you are doing, even if you do not know its name. Mist Gathering is the first realm, and every cultivator under heaven climbs it before anything else. It has nine layers, from the first layer to the ninth layer, and each one holds more mist than the one before."

"Which layer am I?" Tamsin asked.

Kovic pressed two fingers to her wrist and listened, as though her pulse were a story. "Tamsin Vey has reached the third layer of Mist Gathering. Not bad for a girl who counts frogs."

She grinned so widely that a frog escaped from the bucket and nobody chased it.

That evening she wrote the words in the back of her copybook: third layer, Mist Gathering. Then she lay awake and listened to the reeds, trying to hear whether they were saying anything about the fourth.

The older disciples laughed at her the next morning, because they could all see the ink stains on her fingers and guessed what she had written. One of them, a tall boy called Dace Mullery, told her that nobody stayed proud of the third layer for long. She decided she did not like him, and then decided she would remember that he was probably right.
