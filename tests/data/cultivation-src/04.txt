The Roots Take Hold

In the first thaw Tamsin went alone to the reed pond, before the sun was up, and sat in water so cold it burned. She did not draw any more mist. Instead she did what Elder Kovic Brannoch had described only once, at the far end of a long dinner: she let the mist she already held sink downward, and she followed it.

It went into her bones. It went into her feet and out through her soles into the mud of the pond, and for a moment she could feel every reed in the valley as though they were her own hair. Then it came back to her, thicker, and wound through her limbs like ivy round a post.

When she opened her eyes, Kovic was standing on the bank, and so was Dace Mullery, both of them soaked to the knees.

"Say it," Dace said to the elder. "I want to hear it said."

Kovic waded out and took her wrist, and he held it longer than he ever had before. "Tamsin Vey has broken through to the early stage of Root Binding," he said. "One year and one thaw from the third layer of Mist Gathering. I have not seen it done faster in this valley."

Tamsin found that she was crying, and that the water around her had gone warm.

That afternoon Kovic sat with her on the drying rack and told her, for the first time, what lay beyond. "Pearl Forming is the third realm. There the roots you have grown draw everything into a single pearl, and a cultivator who holds one can stand in a flood and stay dry. Nobody in this sect has formed a pearl in forty years."

"Then someone should," Tamsin said.

She wrote the fourth line in her copybook that night: early stage, Root Binding. Below it, very small, she wrote Pearl Forming, and then she closed the book before she could think better of it.
