The Seventh Layer

All summer Tamsin waded in the reed pond before dawn and sat on the drying rack after dusk, breathing mist until her lips turned blue. Elder Kovic Brannoch came and went, saying very little, tapping his silent flute against his palm.

In the first week of autumn the mist in her belly stopped feeling like weather and began to feel like stone. She woke one night with frost on her blanket and a sound in her ears like a bell rung under water.

Kovic was waiting outside her door when she opened it, as if he had known. He took her wrist again.

"Tamsin Vey has reached the seventh layer of Mist Gathering," he said. "Four layers in one summer. Do not let it go to your head. Heads are where the mist leaks out."

She asked what came after the ninth layer, because she had been wanting to ask for months.

"After Mist Gathering comes Root Binding. Root Binding is the second realm, and there the mist you gather is bound into roots that run through the whole body. Root Binding has an early stage, a middle stage and a late stage. Most disciples of this sect die old in the early stage of it, and they are content."

"I will not be content," Tamsin said.

"No," Kovic agreed. "You will be tired."

Dace Mullery heard about the seventh layer by breakfast. He came to her table with his bowl and said that he had stood in the early stage of Root Binding since the spring, and that the gap between them was wider than the valley. Tamsin said that valleys could be crossed. He laughed, but he did not tell her she was wrong, and he sat with her for the rest of the meal talking about the pond and the frogs.

That night she wrote a new line in the copybook, under the old one: seventh layer, Mist Gathering. She looked at the empty page below it for a long time before she slept, and she did not write anything there.
