The Quiet Traveller

The thaw came late, and with it a traveller nobody had seen climb the stair. He was simply there one morning, sitting on the rim of the courtyard fountain, a lean grey man with a wooden flute tucked into his belt and no pack at all.

"Hollis Grey," he said, when Mara asked his name. "I am passing through. I usually am."

Toven Rusk did not seem surprised to see him. The two old men sat in the library for most of the day with the door shut, and when they came out Toven looked thoughtful and Hollis looked exactly the same as he had that morning.

Mara found him on the wall at dusk, listening to the wind come up the valley.

"You hear it too," she said. "The singing at the bend in the stair. I did not hear it the day I came, but I hear it now."

"Of course you do. You stand in Emberheart now, and your ears have caught up with your hands." He smiled. "I hear it because I am a Dusk Listener. Dusk Listener is the first realm of the Vesper Hollow, a sect that keeps no mountain and no hall. We walk, and we listen, and once in a long while we stop on a wall like this one and talk to a girl who asks too many questions."

"How many realms does your sect have?"

"More than I will ever reach, and fewer than the stories say. There is an older sect called Dusk Listener as well, and its first realm is the Dusk Listener, just as yours is Ashwalker." He took the flute from his belt and turned it over in his fingers. "I came to tell Toven that the Glass Lantern Sect has closed its gates. Nobody knows why. Your friend with the empty lanterns may not come back this way for a long time."

He left before the morning bell. Mara went down to the gate to see him off and found only the empty bench and the clay bowl, full of shelled beans, waiting for whoever would sit there next.

That evening she opened the sect ledger and wrote a second line beneath her first: Emberheart, in the summer rains.
