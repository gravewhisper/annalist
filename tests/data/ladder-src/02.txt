Two Kinds of Ash

On the first rest day Mara asked Toven Rusk the question that had been bothering her since the gate. The older students said they belonged to Ashwalker, and Toven had told her she stood in the Ashwalker realm. She could not see how one word could be both a place to stand and a people to belong to.

Toven laughed so hard that he had to sit down on the woodpile.

"You are the first in nine years to ask it out loud," he said. "Listen, then. Ashwalker is the name of our sect, and Ashwalker is also the name of its first realm. The founders were proud and not very imaginative. They named the house after the first door, and everyone since has been too polite to change it."

"So I belong to Ashwalker because I stand in Ashwalker."

"You belong to the sect because you came through the gate and you stayed. You stand in the realm because you can hold the warmth in your heart. A student can leave the sect and still keep the realm, and a cook can serve the sect for forty years and never reach the realm at all. Old Pell in the kitchen has done exactly that, and he makes the best bread on the mountain."

Mara thought about this for the rest of the day while she split kindling. The sect had a hall, a library with a leaking roof, fourteen students, two elders, and a goat that nobody owned. The realm had none of those things. It was only the warmth, and the knowledge of how to keep it.

In the evening Toven showed her a ledger kept by the sect since its founding. Each page held a name, a date, and a single line about the day that student first stood in a realm. Some pages had more lines. Most had only one.

"Write yours," he said, and handed her the pen.

She wrote: Mara Quell, ninth day of the thaw, Ashwalker. Then she sat looking at the empty lines beneath it for a long time, wondering what words would go there.
