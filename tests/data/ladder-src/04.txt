The Pyre Stair

Winter closed the mountain. The students moved their beds into the great hall to share the heat of the central fire, and Toven Rusk began taking Mara up the narrow stair behind the library, the one the others called the Pyre Stair, to a bare room at the very top where the wind came straight through the shutters.

"Sit," he said, the first time. "Do not breathe the Cinder Breath. Do nothing at all. Only notice how cold you are."

She noticed. She was very cold. The warmth in her body wanted to rise and fight it, and she had to hold it back like a dog on a short lead. After an hour she was shaking. After two she had stopped shaking, which frightened her more.

"Enough," Toven said, and wrapped her in his own coat.

On the walk down she asked him about the old story from the hearth, the woman whose roof the snow would not touch.

"The Pyre Sovereign was real," he said. "She was my teacher's teacher. But the name was never hers alone. The Pyre Sovereign is the third realm of Ashwalker, and the highest one the sect has ever written down. She was the last of us to reach it. That was ninety years ago."

"Why does anyone stop? If there is a third realm, why not a fourth?"

"Because it is very hard, and because the ones who reach the third realm rarely come back down the stair to teach the rest. She did. That is why we still have a sect at all." He stopped on the landing and looked at her. "You are not ready. You will not be ready for years, and perhaps never. But you asked, so now you know where the stair goes."

Mara went to bed that night beside the central fire with the square of clear glass from Sefa Lind in her fist, and she dreamed of a roof with no snow on it.
