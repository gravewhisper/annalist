The Ash Gate

Mara Quell reached the top of Mount Teral on the ninth morning of the thaw, with grey dust in her hair and both knees bleeding through her trousers. The gate at the summit was a ring of black stone, older than any road that led to it, and an old man sat beneath it shelling beans into a clay bowl.

"You climbed the whole stair," he said, without looking up. "Most turn back at the bend where the wind starts singing."

"I did not hear any singing," Mara said.

"Then you were not listening, or you were too stubborn to stop. Either will do." He set the bowl aside and stood. He was taller than she had thought, thin as a rake, with burn scars that ran from his wrists to his elbows. "I am Toven Rusk. I teach the ones who reach the gate."

He taught her the Cinder Breath that same afternoon. It was a slow way of breathing, in through the nose for the count of seven and out through the teeth for the count of nine, while she held a coal in her cupped hands and did not let it burn her. She dropped the coal eleven times. On the twelfth try the heat moved into her palms and stayed there, warm and patient, like a cat settling in a lap.

"Good," Toven said. "That warmth is the start of everything. When it reaches your heart and does not leave, you will stand in the realm we call Ashwalker. It is the first step a body takes on this mountain."

By the end of the week the warmth had reached her heart. Mara stood in the Ashwalker realm, and she could walk barefoot over the banked fires of the kitchen without a single blister.

That night the older students told stories by the hearth. The best of them was about the Pyre Sovereign, a woman who had burned so brightly that the snow on Mount Teral never settled on her roof. Nobody would say whether the Pyre Sovereign was a person, a title, or only a story told to frighten new students into practising.
