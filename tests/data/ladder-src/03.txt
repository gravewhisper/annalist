The Glass Lantern

The visitors came up the stair in the middle of the summer rains, three of them in long coats the colour of wet slate, carrying lanterns made of clear glass that gave no light at all. Their leader was a young woman named Sefa Lind, and she bowed to Toven Rusk as if she had known him all her life.

"The Glass Lantern Sect sends its greetings," she said, "and asks to shelter under your roof until the rain stops."

They stayed for nine days. Sefa was only a year older than Mara, and the two of them took to sparring in the courtyard every morning, Mara with her warm hands and Sefa with a stillness that made her very hard to strike.

"How do you do that?" Mara asked on the fourth morning, after missing for the twentieth time.

"Clear Pane," Sefa said. "Clear Pane is the first realm of the Glass Lantern Sect. You learn to see through a thing instead of at it. My teacher stands much higher than I do. She says the second step is like turning the pane into a prism, but she will not tell me its name until I am ready to hear it."

Mara tried to see through Sefa and only gave herself a headache. But something else happened during those nine days. The warmth in her heart, which had sat quietly since the thaw, began to spread outward into her arms and down into her legs, until on the last night she could not sleep for the heat of it.

Toven found her at dawn sitting in the snowmelt of the courtyard fountain, steam rising from her shoulders.

"There it is," he said, and he looked prouder than she had ever seen him. "Emberheart. Emberheart is the second realm of Ashwalker, and you reached it before your first winter on the mountain."

Sefa Lind left the next day with her empty lanterns. Before she went she pressed a small square of clear glass into Mara's hand and told her to look through it when she felt lost.
