Gale Reader

The secret came out at the spring storms. Corrow climbed to the top of the sea wall in the worst of the wind, and Iselde, who had followed him against orders, heard him shout the name of the circle into the gale.

He did not come down for an hour. When he did, he was soaked to the skin and shaking, and he walked straight past her into the kitchen without a word. The cook took one look at him and put the kettle on, and then she took a second look at Iselde, dripping in the doorway, and put on a second kettle. Neither of them scolded her, which frightened Iselde more than any scolding could have done.

Later, wrapped in blankets by the kitchen fire, he told her. "Circle 3: Gale Reader. It is the last circle of Saltmere that any of us has reached. A Gale Reader can hear tomorrow's weather in today's wind."

"And below the Gale Reader?" Iselde asked.

"Drowned Choir is Circle 4 of the Saltmere School, and that is only a story for frightening students."

Iselde Varn, Circle 1, sat very still and did not ask how many years it had taken him. She thought she already knew the answer, and that it was more years than she had lived.

The storms went on for nine days. The sea came over the wall twice and filled the boathouse, and the students spent a whole night bailing the long room with buckets and cooking pots while the shutters banged above their heads. Nobody slept. In the grey hour before dawn the cook brought up bread and hot milk, and they sat on the wet floor and ate it with their fingers, too tired to talk. When the weather finally broke, the beach was covered in weed and broken crates and one enormous, bewildered fish, and the whole school went down to look at it. Corrow said it was a good omen. The cook said it was supper. Iselde, standing between them with her boots full of sand, felt the tide turn in her wrists and laughed out loud for no reason she could name.
