The Salt Gate

Iselde Varn came to the Saltmere School at the end of the herring season, with a borrowed coat and a letter she could not read. The school stood on a spit of black rock where the tide ran twice a day through the lower cellars, and the porter made her wait in the spray until a teacher came down.

The teacher was Corrow Penhallow, a broad man with salt crusted in his beard. He read her letter twice and then folded it into his sleeve.

"Every school on this coast is a ladder of circles," he said. "A student climbs one circle at a time, and each circle has a name of its own. Ours begins at Circle 1: Tidewatcher. A Tidewatcher can feel the tide turn in her own blood, even three rooms from the sea."

That evening he gave her a cup of grey liquid that smelled of kelp and iron. "This is the Tidewatcher elixir," he said. "Drink it slowly. The circle is the rank, and the elixir is only the key to the door."

She drank it slowly. By morning she could feel the tide in her wrists, and she stood in the first circle of Saltmere.

The other students were not impressed. There were eleven of them, most older than she was, and they slept in a long room above the boathouse where the wind came in through the shutters and the blankets were never quite dry. They taught her where to hang her coat, which stair creaked, and how to eat porridge quickly before the gulls came in through the kitchen window. Nobody asked about the letter. On the third night a tall boy named Aubin showed her how to wrap her feet in oiled wool so that the cellar floods would not numb them, and she decided, without telling him, that she would stay.
