Kestrel and Thornwick

A letter came from Hennet with the first swallows. Iselde had learned her letters over the winter, so Corrow let her read it aloud by the kitchen fire while the cook shelled peas.

"Two new schools have opened in the hills," Hennet wrote. "The first calls itself Kestrel, after the first door its students pass, and every student of Kestrel begins as a Kestrel. The Circle 2 that follows Kestrel is Hawk Warden, and no student has reached it yet."

"Of the second school I know less. They keep only two doors so far, and they teach Circle 1 behind a locked gate. Its students boast of it in the market: Circle 1: Thornwick, the hardest door on the coast. Their Circle 2 is Bramble Sage, but nobody has brewed its elixir."

Corrow snorted at that and said that every new school thought its first door was the hardest, and the cook said that every old man thought the same about his own. Iselde folded the letter and kept it in her pocket with the candle stub.

The rest of the letter was about smaller things. The heron that nested on the Gloam roof had come back for its ninth year, and the cook there had burned the bread twice in one week, which Hennet called a scandal. The marsh path had flooded at the second bridge, so anyone who wished to visit would have to wait for the summer or learn to swim. At the bottom of the page, in a smaller hand, Hennet asked whether Iselde had lit the candle yet, and told her not to wait too long, because luck kept no better than milk.

That night Iselde sat on the cellar stair with her feet in the cold water and thought about the two new schools. She tried to picture a student standing at a locked gate, waiting for a door that everyone in the market called the hardest on the coast, and she found that she envied them a little. Saltmere had no boasts. It had herring, and nets, and a teacher who slept over the flood. When the tide turned in her wrists she counted it as she always did, slowly, and went up to bed before the gulls woke.
