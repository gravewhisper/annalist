Four Circles

Hennet stayed three days. On the second night she sat with Iselde by the cellar stair and drew four rings in the wet sand with her finger.

"The Gloam School keeps four circles, and every student learns them by heart. Circle 1: Gloam. Circle 2: Wickbearer. Circle 3: Night Cantor. Circle 4: Ember Abbot. I stand at the third, and I do not expect to climb any higher."

Iselde said the names over to herself under her breath, the way she had once learned the names of the boats in her village. They sounded old and a little sad, like words from a song that nobody sang any more. She asked whether every student of the hills learned the same names, and Hennet said that they did, and that some of them learned nothing else, which was a pity, because there were also sums and letters and the proper way to cook an eel.

"Why not?" Iselde asked.

"Because an Ember Abbot never sleeps, and I am fond of sleeping." Hennet rubbed the rings away with her palm. "Your Saltmere has only three circles that anyone admits to, and the third is a secret Corrow keeps even from me."

On the third morning Hennet left before dawn. Iselde walked with her to the end of the spit, carrying her bag, and they stood together for a while watching the fishing boats go out with their lamps still lit. The marsh lay grey and flat beyond the water, and somewhere in it, Hennet said, was a path that only the herons knew. She gave Iselde a stub of candle wrapped in a scrap of oiled cloth and told her to light it on the darkest night of the year, for luck. Then she pulled her black cloak around her shoulders and went down the causeway without looking back. Iselde kept the candle in her pocket all winter, and she did not light it, because she was afraid that the luck would be used up.
