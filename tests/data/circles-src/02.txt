Brine Warden

Corrow Penhallow did not sleep in a bed. He slept in a hammock slung over the flooded cellar, and in the mornings he walked up the stair with his boots full of seawater and his temper no better for it.

The cook was a small, quick woman with red forearms and a voice that could be heard over the surf. She had worked at the school for thirty years and had opinions about everyone in it, most of them unkind and all of them accurate.

"He is a Brine Warden," said the cook, when Iselde asked. "Brine Warden is Circle 2 of the Saltmere School. They say a Brine Warden can hold a wave still for as long as he holds his breath."

The Brine Warden elixir was kept in a locked chest in the cellar, and only Corrow had the key. Iselde saw it once: a stoppered flask of green glass with a crust of salt around the neck.

She practised feeling the tide every day for a month. Corrow said nothing about her progress, which the cook told her was the highest praise he gave.

The work was dull and cold. Each morning she sat on the lowest step of the cellar stair with her bare feet in the water and counted the slow pull of the tide until her toes went white. Each afternoon she mended nets with the other students, because the school paid for its bread with herring, and herring did not catch themselves. In the evenings the cook let her scrape the porridge pot, and told her stories about the storms of her own girlhood, when the sea had come over the wall and filled the kitchen to the height of the table. By the end of the month Iselde could tell high water from low with her eyes shut and her hands in her lap, and she could mend a torn net faster than Aubin, which pleased her more than she would admit.
