# How a build works

`annalist build` runs these stages in order. `STAGES` in `annalist/build.py` defines them. Each stage also exists as its own subcommand, such as `python3 -m annalist relate runs/book`.

1. `extract` splits the epub into chapter text files and writes `chapters.json`, `source.json`, and `volumes.json`.
2. `book` writes `book.json`: the power system, the power types, extra character fields, the timeline kind, and the power ladder shape.
3. `read` reads each chapter in order and writes `state/NNNN.json` with that chapter's entries, facts, relations, and open questions.
4. `timelines` labels which timeline each chapter happens in. It is skipped when `book.json` says the story is linear.
5. `wordings` splits relation wordings from older runs into a predicate and a detail.
6. `retype` checks the type each entry is filed under. An entry filed under the ladder's group type that a fact or relation states is a numbered or ordered rank, such as "X is Circle 2 of Y", moves to the rank type, unless the wiki also gives it ranks of its own. So does an entry of an item type named after ranks (the type of "X elixir" when X is a rank or group) that a fact or relation states is a group's numbered or ordered rank, such as "The Circle 2 elixir of Y is X", unless its own name ends in the item word.
7. `discover` adds entries for names that recur in the text but that `read` never listed.
8. `resolve` merges entries that are one person, place, or thing under different names, including identities the book reveals later.
9. `relate` adds relations that each chapter shows between entries it names close together.
10. `link` adds relations that possessive names and character facts state.
11. `leads` folds a question raised again within two chapters of an open question about the same mystery into the open one, then keeps rejected answers to a question that are still useful partial leads.
12. `sweep` searches later chapters for passages that answer each open question.
13. `tiers` labels each question's scope as `series`, `arc`, or `cliffhanger`, and checks that it is a mystery. A question that only asks what a character will say or do next, how someone will react, or the reason for a passing gesture such as a bow is not one: `answers` and `ending` skip it, and the wiki and site leave it out.
14. `profile` fills each character's infobox and profile from quoted facts.
15. `ladders` ranks the steps of each power ladder. It is skipped when the book has no ladder. When the ladder steps through the rank type, the model may also place a step that no entry holds, such as the first rank of a group named after it. Such a step becomes an entry of the rank type, dated at its quote, when the quote verifies in its chapter and names both the step and the book's rank word. A step that shares its name with a group gets an entry of its own. The evidence also lists each passage of the read chapters where the rank word and a number stand next to a capitalized name, as in "Circle 2: Warden", "Warden, Circle 2" or "Circle 2 was called Warden", or where the name stands alone as the next sentence before a description that opens with a pronoun, so ranks the read skipped, such as a whole school's ranks listed in one paragraph, can reach the ladder. Two entries the ladder places at the same rank of one group, such as a rank's old and new names, go to the `resolve` judge as one pair, with the passages that name both as evidence. When the judge finds them the same and a quote equates them, they merge from the later of that quote's chapter and the chapter that places the second one. Entries of different groups are never paired.
16. `families` sorts relation wordings into kinds, which set the graph's line colours.
17. `answers` judges, at each volume end, which open questions are answered, narrowed by leads, or based on a false premise. A question is shown the rest of the chapter that raised it and the later chapters that bear on it most. It is also shown one passage from each of up to 4 of the 30 chapters before it, so a clue that came before the question can be a lead. Such a lead shows from the question's chapter on.
18. `ending` judges the questions still open at the end of the book. It runs only when `book.json` sets `complete` and every chapter is read.
19. `volumes` divides the chapters read into story arcs and writes each finished volume's overview, arcs, and key events. Every arc but the last of the volume in progress is closed, and `arcs.json` keeps each closed arc frozen: a later build with more chapters read divides only the chapters after it, and the finished volume keeps it. Each closed arc is named and summarised from its own chapters' synopses only.
20. `proofread` reviews the wiki of each finished volume the way a reader who knows the whole volume would. Each session gets the volume's full text, then one slice of the wiki as the pipeline built it at the volume's last chapter: the characters, the entries of the book's own types with the ladders, the places, organizations, and other entries, or the questions. A slice larger than about 100,000 tokens is split by a hash of each entry's name, so an entry keeps its slice while the number of slices stays the same. A volume longer than about 850,000 tokens is read in consecutive parts, and each slice is reviewed against each part. The model proposes corrections in the formats of `corrections.json`. Code keeps a proposal only when each chapter it gives lies in the text the session saw, each quote is found in its chapter, each name matches an entry or question, and its answer, value, title, or wording uses no word that the book first uses after its chapter. The kept rows go to `proofread.json`, and every rejected row is listed there with its reason. See [Correct a wiki](corrections.md#corrections-the-proofread-stage-proposes).
21. `render` writes page summaries, a History paragraph for each story arc in which a character has at least 5 quoted facts, and a Markdown and HTML wiki under `wiki/`. Each paragraph names the page by the name the site shows at the paragraph's chapter: a summary's chapter, the last chapter of a History arc, or the last chapter of the evidence for Personality and Appearance. So a page that starts as a description, such as "the Tide Reader expert", keeps that description in its paragraphs before the chapter that reveals the page's name, and uses the name from that chapter on.
22. `export` writes the static site to `site/`.
23. `review` writes `review.md` and `review.json`, a list of problems that code can find, such as merges across types, characters who die and then come back, and ranks the text names with the book's rank word that no ladder holds.

The ladder that `render` and `export` show is `ladders.json` plus every entry of the rank type, after `types.json`, `corrections.json` and `proofread.json` set its type, that a fact or relation places at a numbered or ordered rank of a group ("X is Circle 4 of Y", "X | is the first circle of | Y") and that no ladder holds yet. Such a step dates from the chapter that states it. An entry whose name ends in an item word stays off the ladder.

A build is resumable. If a stage fails, the build exits with status 2, and running the same command again continues from where it stopped. Six layers keep finished work:

- `read` skips every chapter that already has a `state/NNNN.json`.
- `build.json` stamps each finished stage with a digest of its input files and a digest of the code it runs. A stage reruns only when one of the two changes. `--dry-run` shows the reason, such as `run: code changed`.
- `cache/` stores every LLM reply, keyed by the step, the backend, the model, and the prompt. A rerun with the same prompt reuses the reply and costs nothing.
- `relate-replies.json` keeps each chapter's `relate` reply. A rebuild reuses it unless the chapter changed or the chapter names more than 2 new pairs of entries. Renames do not count as new pairs.
- `answers.json` keeps a question's verdict through a rebuild unless the question or its leads changed, or more than 2 of the chapters it was shown changed. Small shifts in which chapters rank highest do not ask it again.
- `proofread` makes one call per session, and the cache keys each call by its whole prompt. A rerun asks again only for a slice whose wiki changed. The `--budget` check also runs before each `proofread` session that the cache cannot answer, because one session costs about as much as reading the volume once.

Every LLM call is logged to `calls.jsonl` with its tokens and cost. The `--budget` check sums those costs.
